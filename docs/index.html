SeedGenius Europe: Wheat Variety Selector

Choose a Swiss trial site and the kind of season you expect. The model estimates how good the season will be and ranks the winter wheat varieties tested in recent years. It is built from 34 years of official Agroscope variety trials (1990–2023) with weather for every site and season.

Sites are coded (L1–L16) in the public data.

How good are the picks?

Each season from 1990 to 2023 was predicted by models trained only on the other 33 seasons. Estimating variety merit with a proper two-way model picks the best variety far more often than raw averages or a machine-learning model with weather. Adding weather did not improve the choice itself, so the ranking mostly reflects genetics, while weather sets how good the season will be.

What if the summer is hotter? Testing many possible seasons

Instead of one guess, the app replays every past season at the chosen site (1990–2023), shifted by the warming you set, and adds the random variation seen in the trials. That gives about 2,000 possible seasons. For each variety it reports its chance of being the best, its expected yield and its range from a bad year to a good year.

Can these chances be trusted?

Tested on 34 seasons the model had not seen: when it gave a variety a certain chance of winning a trial, the variety won about that often. Above 70% it is slightly overconfident (based on only 23 cases). The warming itself is an assumption, and the weather model is linear, so it cannot capture sudden heat-stress damage above the temperatures seen in 1990–2023.
